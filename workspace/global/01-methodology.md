# Translation methodology

## Terminology

- Every game term (components, actions, phases, mechanics, keywords, card names) has exactly one
  Czech equivalent, recorded in the project glossary. Use it everywhere, including headings,
  examples, icons' captions and the component list.
- Score-type words are a classic trap: decide once (e.g. *body* vs *skóre*) and stick to it.
- Proper names of characters/places follow the glossary; if the glossary says "keep English", keep it.

## Emphasis conventions

- **Bold** is used for: defined game terms on first use in a section, keywords on cards, and
  phase names in the round structure.
- *Italic* is used for: examples of play ("*Příklad:* …"), flavour text, and references to
  other sections ("viz *Konec hry*").
- Never combine bold and italic. Never bold a whole sentence.
- Capitalisation: game terms are lower-case in running text unless the glossary marks them as
  capitalised (e.g. card titles).

## Typography (Czech)

- Czech quotation marks „ “, en dash with spaces for ranges and asides, non-breaking space after
  one-letter prepositions (k, s, v, z, o, u, a, i) and before units/numbers where required.
- Numbers: "3 body", not "3body"; thousands with a thin/non-breaking space.

## Structure

- Keep the section structure and numbering of the original. Every heading in the original must
  have a counterpart in the translation.
- Example boxes, sidebars and icon legends are part of the text and must be checked too.
